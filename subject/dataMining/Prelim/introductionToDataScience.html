1. What's so important about data? The Big Three

The three teams that sit around the data in most organisations:

  1. Data Team: Solves a business problem and does a significant amount of work on the data that is available first.
  2. Business Intelligence Team: Provides business insights through dashboards and reporting.
  3. Data Science Team: Uses business analytics or data analytics tools to build models that predict future outcomes.

3. Data Analysis vs Data Analytics

Two words people use interchangeably. They are not the same thing.

What is Data Analysis?

  1. Break it down: Divide a dataset into manageable chunks, study them individually, then examine how they relate to the other parts.
  2. Look at what happened: You are working with events that have already taken place.
  3. Answer How and Why: The output is an explanation, not a prediction.

What is Data Analytics?

  1. Explore what's ahead: Analytics is aimed at potential future events.
  2. Apply reasoning: Logical and computational reasoning applied to the component parts you obtained in an analysis.
  3. Turn patterns into action: Look for patterns, then explore what you could do with them going forward.

4. Qualitative & Quantitative Analytics

Two ways of getting to an answer:

  • Qualitative (Intuition + Analysis): Human judgment applied to what the data shows.
  • Quantitative (Formulas + Algorithms): Structured methods that scale beyond what a person can hold in mind.

5. Business Analytics

Analytics applied specifically to business data, aimed at a decision rather than at discovery for its own sake.

  • What it works with: Sales, operations, marketing, and finance data. The everyday records a business already generates.
  • What it asks: How are we performing, what is driving it, and what is likely to happen next quarter.
  • What it produces: Dashboards, reports, and forecasting models that a manager can actually act on.
  • Where it sits: The bridge between the BI team's reporting and the data science team's predictive models.

6. Business Intelligence (BI) & BI in Practice

Business Intelligence (BI): The process of analysing and reporting historical business data.

  • Aims to explain past events: Uses business data.
  • Backward looking: It reports what already happened, it does not forecast.
  • Shared view: Everyone reads performance from the same numbers.
  • BI Dashboard: Historical performance, read at a glance.

Business Intelligence in Practice

Includes all technology-driven tools involved in the process of analyzing, understanding and reporting available past data (BI Report, BI Dashboard):

  • Make decisions: On evidence, rather than on instinct or the loudest voice.
  • Extract insights: Work out what the numbers are actually saying.
  • Extract ideas: Surface new questions worth chasing next.
Knowledge Check

Question: Which of the following best describes business intelligence (BI)?

Correct Answer: Analysing and reporting historical business data to explain past events.

7. Machine Learning (ML) & How It Works

Machine Learning (ML): The ability of machines to predict outcomes without being explicitly programmed.

ML is about creating and implementing algorithms that let machines receive data and use this data to:

  • Make predictions: Estimate what is likely to happen next, from data it has already seen.
  • Analyse patterns: Find structure in volumes of data far too large to eyeball.
  • Give recommendations: Suggest the next action, product, or route worth taking.

Machine learning is the engine behind the predictive side of data science.

How Machine Learning Works

Formula: Mathematics + Computing power + AI (Artificial intelligence) = Predict outcomes from data without being explicitly programmed to.

The algorithm, step by step: Training data → Model → Minimise the error → Predictions.

AI + statistics = Predictions with unprecedented accuracy.

8. Artificial Intelligence (AI): Traditional vs Generative AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI): Simulating human knowledge and decision making with computers.

  • We, as humans, have only managed to reach AI through machine learning.
  • Judgment, not just calculation: The aim is decisions a person would otherwise have to make.
  • Learned, not hand-written: The rules come from data, which is why ML is the route in.
  • Machine learning is the part of AI we have actually built.

Traditional AI vs Generative AI

Two different jobs, both sitting under the same AI umbrella:

  • Traditional AI: Mainly applied to structured datasets to improve efficiency and accuracy in repetitive tasks. (Examples: Forecasting, fraud detection, recommendation ranking).
  • Generative AI: Generating new raw data or content, such as text, code, audio, or images, by relying on patterns from a given training dataset. (Examples: Chat assistants, image generation, code completion).

9. Data & Data Science at a Glance Timeline & Needs

When Each One Is Applied

  • Traditional and big data: At the beginning of your analysis.
  • Business intelligence: After the data has been gathered and organised.
  • Traditional methods and ML: After BI reports have been created and discussed.

Why You Need Each One

  • Traditional and big data: Data-driven decisions require well-organised and relevant raw data stored in a digital format.
  • Business intelligence: Use data to create reports and dashboards to gain business insights.
  • Traditional methods: Assess potential future scenarios by using advanced statistical methods.
  • Machine learning: Utilise artificial intelligence to predict behaviour in unprecedented ways.

10. Traditional Data vs Big Data

Traditional Data

The everyday, orderly kind. Most businesses run on it.

  • Structured: Rows and columns with a fixed shape you can count on.
  • Managed from one computer: No cluster needed. It fits in a single database on a single machine. One machine is enough.

Big Data

Too much, too varied, and too fast for a single machine to hold. Stored and processed across many machines.

  • Structured: Tidy rows and columns, the same shape as traditional data.
  • Semi-structured: Tags or markers but no fixed table. Think JSON or XML.
  • Unstructured: Text, images, audio, video. No inherent shape at all.

The Vs of Big Data

  • 3 Vs (The original three almost everyone agrees on): Volume, Variety, Velocity.
  • 5 Vs, 7 Vs, 11 Vs (Added along the way): Value, Variability, Visualisation, Vision.

11. Traditional Methods

Perfect for forecasting future performance with great accuracy (Predictive Analytics).

  • Regression: Fit a line through the data to estimate one value from another.
  • Cluster analysis: Group observations that behave alike, without being told the groups.
  • Factor analysis: Reduce many variables down to the few underlying drivers.

12. Data Science Process (5 Steps)

Data science is a discipline reliant on data availability, while business analytics does not completely rely on data. It is mostly the part that uses complex mathematical, statistical, and programming tools.

Every earlier stage still lives inside it. Data science is simply the name the industry settled on.

The Algorithm / Process, Step by Step:

  1. Ask: Start from a real question, not from the data you happen to have.
  2. Gather and clean: Collect the data and get it into a state you can trust.
  3. Explore: Apply statistical methods and find the patterns that matter.
  4. Model: Build something that forecasts, classifies, or ranks.
  5. Act: Put the result in front of the person who has to decide.
